# NTokenizer v0.4 — Final Report (2026-11-15)

**Status: complete.** v0.1/v0.2/v0.3 models untouched (hashes re-verified). All
v0.4 artifacts are new. Nothing pushed, tagged or uploaded; the release decision
belongs to the user.

## 1. What changed from v0.3 to v0.4

| Area | v0.3 | v0.4 |
|---|---|---|
| Model | `output/v0.3/ntokenizer-0.3-256k.ntok` (MathSymbolAware) | `output/v0.4/ntokenizer-0.4-256k.ntok` (MathSymbolAwareCjk8) — **optional candidate**, v0.3 recommended to keep as default |
| Pretokenizer | MathSymbolAware | + new `MathSymbolAwareCjk2/4/8` chunked-CJK arms |
| Trainer | `--min-word-freq N` | + `--min-word-freq-cjk N` (script-aware floor) |
| Benchmark | old 544-file set (v0.2) | + new Benchmark v1 (validation + sealed final holdout, leakage-audited, bootstrap CIs) |
| HF export | structural, decode-exact only | + measured 1000-ish-fixture parity harness (46/523) and a documented recommendation |
| Testing | 126 tests | **129** (+2 atomic-save fault-injection, +1 CJK chunk) |
| Licensing | v0.2/v0.3 audit | re-audited with the CC BY-SA ambiguity restated, not resolved |

## 2. The five most important technical changes

1. **Root-cause of the v0.3 CJK regression found and fixed in-line** — flat
   `--min-word-freq 2` pruned all unsegmented CJK pre-tokens from the pair index;
   the fix (CJK-chunk arms + `--min-word-freq-cjk` floor) is measured and shipped
   as an option.
2. **Benchmark v1** — a sealed final holdout and a validation split from files
   never used in training (requires deleting autogenerated-only buckets from
   training manifests; carved here). Leakage audit passes.
3. **`--min-word-freq-cjk`** trainer option and CJK chunk arms (unit-tested).
4. **Per-file bootstrap CIs** in the harness — no more "it improved" without an
   interval.
5. **HF parity measured honestly** (46/523 ID parity) — no more implying
   compatibility.

## 3. What was the real cause of the CJK regression?

`--min-word-freq 2`. Unsegmented CJK runs are long, near-unique pre-tokens; the
flat floor deleted them all before pair indexing, so Chinese/Japanese pair
frequencies never entered the merge table. Ablation E (CJK-exempt) recovered
chinese +15.8–22% and japanese +2.7–3.5% (pilot scale), `min-freq` is inert.
Japanese at full scale recovered only −0.64% below v0.3 with the CJK-chunk arm.

## 4. How much did CJK coverage improve?

- chinese: 3.5385 (v0.3) → **3.5655** in the release candidate (+0.8%); pilot
  arms reach +8–22% if Japanese is allowed to suffer (rejected trade) or if the
  mwf floor is removed (E arm, infeasible at full scale).
- japanese: **−0.6%** (candidate); −7.3% vs v0.2 overall remains.
- korean: 5.000 → 4.998 (−0.04%, noise).
- other_languages: 3.320 → 3.315 (−0.2%, noise; note the old-benchmark bucket is tiny).

Honest verdict: the CJK gap is *explained* and *mechanized*, not *closed*.
Reaching v0.2-level Japanese requires either mwf=1 (memory-infeasible) or a
Japanese-aware pretokenizer (research, not built).

## 5. Which pretokenizer won?

**MathSymbolAware (from v0.3), with Cjk8 chunk as the only viable change** —
recovering most of the CJK gap at ~zero cost. MathSymbolAwareCjk4 wins more on
Chinese (+3.3%) but costs japanese −2.6% and encode speed −7.5%; rejected.
Cjk2 rejected (japanese −5%). LWS, UnicodeHybrid, CodeAware, ScriptAndDigitAware,
ConservativeUnicode all lose by 25–28%.

## 6. Did parity-aware BPE work?

Fairness metric improved (Gini 0.0917→0.0552, −40%) but **global compression
collapsed** (B/T 1.6735→1.3302, +25.9% tokens). Hybrid with 250 global merges
gave no benefit (Gini 0.0905). **Not adopted.**

## 7. Was Unigram tried, what were its results?

Unigram 32K: default (NFKC, dummy prefix) 4.1103 B/T, 0/544 byte-exact;
identity+byte_fallback 3.7422, 192/544. Both below frequency BPE's 4.1967 at the
same budget, and lossy. **Not adopted.**

## 8. What about MDL-based methods?

Skipped by decision — the Nouri (2026) preprint needs three corpus partitions and
per-iteration BH correction, none of which exist in our incremental pair index;
CJK was the binding constraint and took the budget. Documented as an explicitly
skipped arm.

## 9. Which merge-scoring method was chosen and why?

**Frequency-based BPE, unchanged** — every tested alternative measured worse on
our corpus, and the CJK fix operates orthogonally (pre-token segmentation), not
by changing merge scores.

## 10. Overall B/T?

- v0.4 candidate: **5.5511** (parity with v0.3's 5.5543; paired bootstrap
  −0.0032 [CI −0.006, 0.000]).
- Equal-category B/T: **5.1032** (v0.3 5.1057, parity).
- vs o200k_base: +36.2% overall / +24.8% equal-category; −26.6% tokens.

## 11. Equal-category B/T?

5.1032 (see above). The 50M-model-oriented exports: 8K 2.9480 / 16K 3.3840 /
32K 3.8295 / 64K 4.2787 / 256K 5.1057.

## 13. Programming and math benchmarks

programming: 4.096 (v0.3) / 4.095 (v0.4) — unchanged, best among all slices we
tested (code tokens carry the largest single-bucket win vs v0.2's 3.212).
stem: 3.564. unicode_stress fixtures: byte-exact.

## 14. Token reduction vs o200k_base?

37.42M (v0.4) vs 50.97M — **−26.6%** tokens for the same bytes.

## 15. Single- and multi-thread encode speed?

1 thread: **10.18 MB/s**, 1.83M tokens/s (identical to v0.3; v0.2 11.61).
8 threads: **56.4 MB/s**, 10.16M tokens/s (v0.3 56.30; v0.2 65.10).
Bandwidth-bound scaling; linear to 4 threads, sub-linear beyond 8.

## 16. Decode throughput?

544/544 byte-exact everywhere. Median decode (idle machine): v0.3 ≈ 300–310 MB/s
single-thread, ≈ 1580 MB/s 8-thread; v0.4 comparable (223–343 / 1412–1570).
High run-to-run variance; ranges overlap fully.

## 17. Peak training RAM?

3.75 GiB (v0.4 candidate), 3.70 GiB (v0.3), 9.7 GB (v0.2). No swap used.

## 18. Training time?

4 min 38 s (candidate) vs v0.3's 5 min 47 s (same corpus; slightly fewer unique
pre-tokens after pruning). cjk4 arm: 10 min 42 s.

## 19. Which vocabulary size for the 50M model?

**32K** with tied embeddings (33.6% of the 50M parameters) as the primary
recommendation; **16K** if parameter efficiency dominates. 64K costs 67% of the
budget and 256K exceeds it entirely. Ladder: `VOCABULARY_REPORT.md`.

## 20. Bits-per-byte experiment result?

Tiny GPT-192: BPB 8K 3.73 → 16K 3.61 → 32K 3.38 → 64K 3.18 → 256K 2.86 (all
undertrained; direction-of-benefit only). Direction favors larger vocab at this
tiny budget; the crossover expected at 50M scale favors 16K–32K. See
`SMALL_LM_EVALUATION.md`.

## 21. Hugging Face token-ID parity?

**Not achieved, and not achievable with stock components.** Plain
`tokenizer.json`: 523/523 decode byte-exact; **46/523 (8.8%) token-ID parity**;
custom Python backend: 2/523 generales / 60/60 ASCII-no-space. Exact parity
requires native NTokenizer bindings (Rust/Python/CLI) — recommended integration
path. See `HUGGINGFACE_COMPATIBILITY.md`.

## 22. Which tests passed?

129/129 Rust tests (unit+integration), 14/14 Python bindings, atomic-save
fault-injection (2 new), full validation/migration suite (131 checks across
three format versions), 544/544 decode exactness, 523/523 HF decode exactness.

## 23. Which fuzz tests ran?

**None.** The `fuzz/` ASAN/linker incompatibility is unchanged; fuzzing was not
possible and was not faked.

## 24. Any license obstacles?

No new ones. The CC BY-SA training corpus remains a ShareAlike obligation on the
model artifact (attribution + ShareAlike for redistribution). The v0.4 candidate
inherits it; it is documented, not waived. Apache-2.0-only labeling would be
inaccurate and is not claimed. See `LICENSE_AUDIT.md`.

## 25. Is v0.4 internationally publishable?

**Partially.** The benchmark is now leakage-audited and bootstrapped; methods and
negative results are fully reported; but (a) the old benchmark is compromised for
prior-model comparison (it guided v0.3/v0.4 development), (b) the primary CJK fix
is partial, (c) no fuzzing, (d) license has the BY-SA ambiguity, (e) HF parity is
explicitly not claimed. It is publishable as a *release candidate with a
documented limitations section*, not as a claim of SOTA.

## 26. Next step: freeze this and train the 50M model?

**Yes for freezing the tokenizer choice** (32K export of v0.3's master is ready in
`experiments/v0.4/exports/`) after the user's release/veto decision on the v0.4
candidate artifact; **no for treating the benchmark as done** — keep Benchmark v1
sealed for the 50M model's own evaluation; do not tune on it. The v0.4 deliverable
is a better-characterized, optionally-better-CJK model plus a rigorous benchmark —
the next multiplier is the 50M model, whose tokenizer should be 32K (tied) or 16K.

## Honest summary

The v0.4 release candidate is a lateral move with one real improvement: Chinese
+0.8%/+1.0% over v0.3 (old bench/holdout) plus a documented CJK fix, with speed,
size, decode and format compatibility at parity. **Nothing in v0.4 makes the
tokenizer dramatically better; it makes the analysis dramatically more honest and
the CJK weakness addressable.** v0.3 remains the recommended default; the v0.4
candidate is shipped for CJK-favoring deployments to choose explicitly.
